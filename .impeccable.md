## Design Context

### Product positioning

**Not "Rover but Spanish." This is a local pet-care ecosystem — owners, sitters, and the neighborhood businesses around them (vets, pet shops, groomers). Rover is marketplace-only; we are community-first. That ecosystem is the moat Rover cannot replicate at scale.**

**Launch scope:** Gijón (Asturias) first → other Asturian cities (Oviedo, Avilés, Langreo) → wider Spain. Each geographic expansion adds a new partner network in that city, reusing the same product. Lifestyle-business framing — €0.3-1M ARR over 3-5 years is success, not a disappointment. The brand must feel locally rooted but not hyper-Gijón-specific — "the neighborhood pet-care network" works in any Spanish city.

**Phase 0 (now → first 20 sitters):** supply-side first. Landing and growth effort biased ~80% toward recruiting sitters + signing partner businesses, ~20% toward owners. Owners can explore and bookmark, but there is nothing to hand them until sitter supply exists.

**Phase 1 (20+ sitters + 5+ partners live):** flip to owner-primary. Partner-discount network becomes the visible differentiator vs. Rover.

### Users

**Four stakeholders, not two:**

- **Sitters (Phase 0 priority).** Local residents wanting side income caring for pets. Our recruitment target, our supply side. They check the app throughout the day between visits, often in low-light / on-the-move contexts. They care about: steady bookings, fair commission, genuinely useful tools (calendar, client records, tax-friendly factura summaries), community.

- **Partner businesses (Phase 0 priority).** Local vets, pet shops, peluquerías caninas, groomers in Gijón. They offer discounts to our users; they get acquisition and visibility in return. This is what Rover doesn't have. Treat them as a first-class audience with their own page and value proposition, not a footer afterthought.

- **Owners (Phase 1 priority).** Spanish dog/pet owners deciding whether to trust a stranger with their home and pet for the first time. Often anxious. Browse on mobile (commute, lunch break) and on desktop (evening research). Care about: verification, trust signals, local presence, and — increasingly — partner perks.

- **Admins.** Internal team verifying DNI/NIE/passport and insurance documents, managing partner relationships, moderating reviews.

**Jobs to be done:**
- **Sitter:** present myself as trustworthy, manage bookings, check in/out of visits, run the business side without a second tool.
- **Partner business:** acquire local customers who've already been pre-trusted by the app; offer them something specific; get listed somewhere locals see.
- **Owner:** find a local, verified sitter I can trust with my pet and home; book and pay without friction; also access neighborhood benefits (vet discounts, shop perks).
- **Everyone:** communicate, review, and build long-term repeat relationships.

### Brand Personality

**Three words:** trustworthy, calm, local.

**Voice:**
- Marketing pages speak from the pet's point of view ("encuentra a alguien que me cuide mientras no estás"). Playful, but never cutesy.
- Functional UI (forms, dashboards, admin, errors) stays human-readable and direct — no mascot voice in places where users need clarity.

**Emotional goal:** A Spanish pet owner leaving their dog for the first time should feel **calm confidence** — the quiet reassurance of a neighborhood service, not the hype of a global marketplace.

### Aesthetic Direction

**Primary reference:** Rover.com, but **modern, calmer, and far less cluttered**. Rover is the benchmark users will compare us to; the design must feel immediately recognizable as "that kind of app," while clearly beating it on breathing room, typography, and confidence.

**Anti-references:**
- Rover's current density — stacks of CTAs, badges, testimonials, cards-on-cards, aggressive promotional banners. We explicitly reject this.
- Generic "pet app" aesthetics — cartoon mascots, squiggles, bubblegum palettes, emoji decoration. No emojis anywhere in the product (already enforced in the codebase; this is a hard rule).
- Global-tech AI-slop look — cyan-on-dark gradients, glassmorphism, gradient text, hero metric layouts, rounded-rectangle card grids.

**What to reach for instead:**
- Editorial calm — generous whitespace, strong typographic hierarchy, one hero element per view, not six competing for attention.
- Spanish neighborhood warmth — palette and imagery should feel local and rooted (warm plaster, ceramic, overcast coastal light), not Silicon Valley.
- Quiet trust cues — verified / insurance / DNI surfaced through restrained typographic labels, not loud shield icons and stripes.

**Geographic portability — the brand must travel beyond Gijón.** Local character at each launch city comes from PHOTOGRAPHY and COPY (neighborhood names, real local partners), not from brand-level motifs. Do not hard-code Asturian-specific symbols (hórreo silhouette, Cruz de la Victoria, Cudillero-specific imagery, cider-pour animations) into the design system — they won't travel to Madrid, Barcelona, or Sevilla. The palette, typography, and principles are intentionally portable across Spanish cities.

**Theme:** Light as the default — owners browsing in daylight, calm coastal feel. A dark mode is acceptable as a later addition for sitters on the move, but is not required for MVP.

**Color freedom:** The existing emerald (`#16a34a`) and warm yellow are NOT locked in. Designers may propose a new palette that better expresses "trustworthy, calm, local, Spanish." Whatever is chosen should:
- Use OKLCH, not hex/HSL, for new tokens.
- Tint neutrals toward the brand hue.
- Avoid the AI palette (neon purple→blue, cyan glow on dark).

### Design Principles

1. **Calm over loud.** Every screen should do fewer things, more confidently. If Rover shows five CTAs on a card, we show one and trust the user to find the rest.
2. **Trust through restraint, not badges.** Verification, insurance, and GPS are our differentiators — surface them typographically and in plain Spanish, never with stripes, shields, or bragging stamps.
3. **Local before global.** Copy, imagery, colors, and examples should feel specifically Spanish (Gijón-first) — not a translated US product. "Cuidador del barrio," not "Your pet's best friend."
4. **Typography carries the hierarchy.** Since we're rejecting card-grid density, the type system must do the heavy lifting. Strong contrast between steps, disciplined scale, one display face paired with one body face.
5. **Functional UI stays human.** Pet-voice lives on marketing pages and empty states. Forms, errors, admin, and dashboards speak plainly and respectfully to adults handling money and home access.
6. **Ecosystem, not marketplace.** Every surface should reinforce that this is a community of four stakeholders, not a two-sided listings site. Partner businesses appear on owner pages. Sitter tools reference real local clients. Owners see which vet partners their sitter has worked with. Make the connections visible — that's the thing Rover's scale cannot fake.
7. **Honest about stage.** No fake stats, no fabricated reviews, no fictional sitter counts. If we have three partners, we show three. If we have eight sitters, we say "únete a los primeros cuidadores de Gijón." Trust compounds when the brand never lies.

### Constraints

- **Stack:** Next.js 16 App Router, React 19, Tailwind CSS 4, Supabase. No shadcn — custom UI library in `src/components/ui/`.
- **Cross-platform future:** A React Native mobile app is on the Phase 2 roadmap. Design tokens, color values, type scale, spacing scale, and component contracts must be expressible **without** web-only mechanisms. Avoid as foundational primitives: `backdrop-blur`, CSS gradients on text, CSS-only hover-driven states, anything that depends on `:has()` or `@container`. Keep these as web *enhancements* only — never the load-bearing version of a component.
- **i18n:** Every string must exist in both `messages/es.json` and `messages/en.json`. Spanish is primary.
- **Icons:** `lucide-react` only — the established two-paw logo is the single exception. No emojis. (Lucide has a React Native package — keep icon usage portable.)
- **Accessibility:** WCAG AA is the baseline. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. Touch targets ≥44×44px (many users are older Spanish pet owners on phones; this is also the iOS / Android HIG minimum and non-negotiable for the future RN app).
- **Performance:** EU edge deployment (Vercel Frankfurt). Map tiles (Leaflet/OSM) are the heaviest asset — treat them as a budget constraint.
